## 6. Extra Credit: Sampling Diversity at Fixed Bits per Byte

### 6.1 Objective

This experiment examined how temperature and nucleus sampling (top-p) affect the diversity of character-level text generated by a fixed language model. I also extended the experiment to investigate whether higher diversity is accompanied by changes in readability, coherence, and repetition.

### 6.2 Experimental Setup

I reused the modern character-level Transformer trained on Tiny Shakespeare in Experiment 3 (512-token context; seed 1337). Its validation bits per byte (bpb) was **2.102364** throughout this experiment. No additional training or model-weight updates were performed.

All samples started from the same prompt, `ROMEO:\n`. I tested temperature values of **0.5, 1.0, and 1.5**, crossed with top-p values of **0.8, 0.9, and 1.0**. Top-k filtering was disabled. For the extended study, each of the nine combinations used **10 generation seeds**, yielding **90 generated passages**, each consisting of 200 new characters. This expanded an initial two-seed pilot to give a more reliable estimate of sampling variability.

Diversity was calculated on the generated suffix only, excluding the prompt:

**Distinct-4 = number of unique overlapping character 4-grams / total overlapping character 4-grams.**

A 200-character output has 197 overlapping 4-grams. I report mean distinct-4 percentages and **sample standard deviations (SD)** across the 10 generated passages at each setting.

For a supplementary qualitative assessment, **27 passages** (three generation seeds per setting) were shuffled and rated by **ChatGPT without displaying the decoding settings**. Ratings used 1–5 scales for readability, coherence, and repetition severity; a higher repetition-severity score indicates more undesirable repetition. These are **AI-assisted, subjective ratings**, not independent human evaluations.

### 6.4 Interpretation

**Temperature.** At fixed top-p = 1.0, mean distinct-4 increased from **86.40%** at temperature 0.5 to **96.34%** at 1.0 and **98.33%** at 1.5. A higher temperature spreads probability across more possible next characters, making outputs less repetitive at the character 4-gram level. However, AI-assisted readability fell from **4.00/5** to **1.33/5** across those same settings, while coherence declined from **3.33/5** to **1.33/5**. The most diverse outputs were not the most understandable.

**Top-p.** Increasing top-p generally increased diversity at each fixed temperature. At temperature 1.0, the mean rose from **90.51%** (top-p 0.8) to **93.86%** (0.9) and **96.34%** (1.0). Including more low-probability candidates can introduce novel sequences, but the accompanying readability ratings also decreased from **3.67/5** to **2.67/5** in these settings. These are descriptive associations across this small set of conditions, not a causal estimate of text quality.

**Repetition and fluency are different.** Repetition-severity ratings remained low across nearly all settings, including highly diverse outputs with poor readability. The generated passage identified as **R03** in the blinded review includes a relatively fluent Shakespeare-like fragment: “That set the house of York, and shall be better.” By contrast, **R10** includes hard-to-interpret forms such as “TouchosNouble san ye. Speak cought, ho!” These illustrative excerpts demonstrate that a passage may contain many distinct 4-grams while still lacking grammatical or semantic quality. The blinded sample IDs are cited here without claiming their decoding conditions because that mapping was not included in the two final summary files.

**Why bpb stayed fixed.** Every condition used the same trained model checkpoint with validation bpb **2.102364**. Temperature and top-p only change the token-selection procedure during generation; they do not alter the model's evaluation probabilities on the held-out dataset. Thus, the fixed bpb refers to the underlying checkpoint, **not** to identical likelihoods for each generated passage.

### 6.5 Limitations

The experiment used one checkpoint, one fixed prompt, and short 200-character passages. Ten samples per condition give a clearer estimate of variability than the original two samples, but the estimates remain noisy. Only three passages per condition received qualitative ratings, all from a single AI evaluator rather than independent human readers. Ratings may reflect evaluator preferences and should not be interpreted as objective measurements of literary quality. Moreover, distinct character 4-grams measure local novelty, not syntactic correctness, coherence, or factual accuracy.

### 6.6 Conclusion

Increasing temperature and relaxing top-p generally raised the fraction of distinct character 4-grams in generated Shakespeare-style text. In the extended experiment, mean diversity ranged from **81.52%** (temperature 0.5, top-p 0.8) to **98.33%** (temperature 1.5, top-p 1.0), a difference of **16.80 percentage points**. The most diverse setting received the lowest AI-assisted readability and coherence ratings (**1.33/5** each). The findings illustrate a practical trade-off: decoding settings can increase surface-level diversity without improving, and sometimes while reducing, perceived text quality. The validation bpb of the trained model remained fixed throughout.
